# Write Zhihu Short Fiction

Write for continuous reader pull without sacrificing causality. Prefer a clean, believable story over extra twists, extra villains, or decorative prose.

## Establish the story contract

Before drafting, determine from the request or make conservative assumptions about:

- genre and reality level;
- target length and delivery type;
- point of view and tense;
- protagonist's concrete goal;
- immediate stakes and irreversible loss;
- the end state that counts as success.

Do not import characters, devices, settings, or plot mechanics from previous unrelated stories. Preserve text and decisions the user has already approved.

## Build the opening hook

Open with a compact 引子 before Chapter 1 when the user requests a full story or a hook-led short story.

Make the hook contain:

1. a concrete abnormal event or conflict;
2. an immediate personal cost;
3. one unanswered causal question.

Keep it brief. Withhold explanation, not basic comprehension. A reader should understand what happened, who is endangered, and why the next paragraph matters.

Avoid:

- background biography before conflict;
- unexplained labels that assume facts the viewpoint character cannot know;
- rhetorical questions with no story consequence;
- stacked mysteries that do not share one causal line;
- a trailer-like summary that spoils the final resolution.

## Design the causal spine

For each major beat, maintain this chain:

`event -> character interpretation -> choice -> consequence -> new information or higher cost`

Do not advance the plot through coincidence after the inciting incident. Every later discovery must come from a choice, observation, mistake, institutional process, or another character's self-interest.

For a complete short story, use these ranges as guidance rather than a rigid formula:

- first 5%: hook and central disturbance;
- 5-20%: first failed response and visible cost;
- 20-50%: investigation, testing, pressure, or relationship escalation;
- 50-75%: deeper cause, moral complication, or revised plan;
- 75-92%: final execution based on accumulated knowledge;
- final 8%: consequence, emotional landing, and residual image.

## Control viewpoint knowledge

At every scene, track:

- current time and location;
- who is physically present;
- what the viewpoint character knows now;
- what each supporting character knows now;
- what each person is hiding, assuming, or misreading;
- how each person obtained any new fact.

Never let a character mention an event that has not happened for them. Do not let the narrator identify a person, death, motive, or object without an observable basis. Do not switch viewpoint merely to explain a hidden plan.

Reveal off-screen plans through something the active viewpoint can access: behavior, records, dialogue, aftermath, contradiction, or a repeatable pattern.

## Write intelligent conflict

Make both protagonist and antagonist adapt.

- Let the protagonist test one variable at a time when uncertainty matters.
- Give the antagonist fallback plans that follow from prior preparation, not unlimited power.
- Make the final plan exploit established habits, incentives, timing, or institutional safeguards.
- Do not make victory depend on a villain confessing, carrying perfectly labeled evidence, forgetting an obvious precaution, or reacting exactly as the protagonist hopes without prior basis.
- Prefer forcing several plausible self-protective choices over delivering a public speech or direct revenge monologue.

Show intelligence through selection and restraint. Do not add paragraphs explaining that the protagonist is intelligent.

## Add emotion through behavior

Tie emotion to the exact event and relationship.

- Replace emotion labels with physical choices, omissions, mistakes, changed speech, or attention to a concrete detail.
- Let shock interrupt normal priorities; let grief alter later decisions; let fear narrow what a person is willing to risk.
- Give supporting characters mixed motives and a boundary they resist crossing.
- Preserve moral responsibility even when a supporting character is coerced or afraid.
- After severe harm, include an appropriate human response before the plot resumes.

Use only the amount of emotion the beat can carry. Do not pause a time-critical scene for a long internal monologue.

## Use short chapters deliberately

Divide chapters when at least one changes:

- objective;
- location or time phase;
- working theory;
- power balance;
- emotional state;
- story question.

End a chapter with a discovery, decision, consequence, or contradiction. Do not force every ending into a one-line aphorism or artificial cliffhanger.

## Add twists only when earned

A twist is optional. Add one only if it:

1. reinterprets earlier facts rather than replacing them;
2. remains consistent with established character motives and knowledge;
3. changes the protagonist's next decision or the meaning of the outcome.

Seed at least two fair clues. Preserve the earlier explanation as reasonable at the time. Reveal the twist through the active viewpoint.

Reject twists that merely reassign the mastermind, excuse an established villain, introduce a hidden relative, or switch viewpoint to announce information. If removing the twist leaves the same climax and emotional result, remove it.

## Resolve the story

The ending must answer the central causal question and complete the protagonist's goal or failure.

- Use knowledge accumulated earlier.
- Pay for victory with effort, risk, sacrifice, or restraint already dramatized.
- Resolve every load-bearing clue.
- Allow minor atmospheric ambiguity only when it does not obscure what actually happened.
- End on a concrete action, image, or changed ordinary detail. Avoid explaining the theme after the image has landed.

## Apply the Zhihu short-fiction voice

- Prefer first person when it strengthens immediacy; otherwise follow the user's viewpoint request.
- Use short paragraphs and varied sentence lengths.
- Let dialogue carry pressure, evasion, bargaining, and unequal knowledge.
- Keep each paragraph focused on one beat.
- Use plain, specific Chinese. Remove promotional phrasing, filler transitions, repeated negation, mechanical three-part lists, and quotable “golden lines” written only to sound profound.
- Limit bolding, ornamental headings, and meta commentary inside the story.
- Do not explain a clue immediately after showing it when the reader can infer its meaning.

## Choose the delivery mode

### Outline

Provide premise, protagonist goal, opposition, hidden causal chain, chapter beats, ending mechanism, clue ledger, and main realism risks.

### Opening or 引子

Deliver the prose directly. Keep it within the requested length and end with forward pressure rather than a summary.

### Full story

Give the title, compact 引子, logically divided chapters, and story only. Add an editorial note or closure audit only when requested.

### Revision

Identify the actual fault, preserve approved material, rewrite the smallest affected span, and propagate necessary time, motive, or clue changes downstream. Do not regenerate unrelated passages unless asked.

### Audit

Report problems in reading order or by severity. Quote the affected line, explain the violated causal or knowledge rule, and offer an exact replacement where useful.

Before delivering a full draft or substantial revision, read [references/logic-audit.md](references/logic-audit.md) and apply it.

Do not include the audit in the response unless the user requests it. Quietly repair issues first.
