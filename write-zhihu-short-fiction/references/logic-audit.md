# Reality and story-logic audit

Use this checklist before delivering a full story, a major rewrite, or a logic diagnosis.

## 1. Scene ledger

For each load-bearing scene, be able to fill this privately:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Time | Does the elapsed time permit every action and journey? |
| Place | How did each character enter, leave, or gain access? |
| Viewpoint | What can the active viewpoint directly observe? |
| Knowledge | How did each spoken fact become known to this speaker? |
| Goal | What does each person want in this scene? |
| Risk | What prevents the easiest action? |
| Change | What new fact, cost, or decision exists afterward? |

Repair any blank field before polishing prose.

## 2. Physical and temporal continuity

Check:

- travel time, traffic, opening hours, queues, security checks, and waiting;
- whether a fleeing vehicle can realistically be followed;
- whether an injured or detained person can perform the next action;
- whether phones, keys, documents, weapons, and clothing remain where the story left them;
- whether simultaneous actions genuinely fit the clock;
- whether a character helps an injured person before pursuing a less urgent objective.

When exact real-world schedules or current rules matter, verify them with authoritative current sources.

## 3. Institutional procedure

For schools, hospitals, police, courts, workplaces, transport systems, and online platforms, check:

- who has authority to detain, search, access, alter, seize, or disclose;
- what records are automatically created;
- what requires consent, a warrant, a supervisor, or a formal report;
- how long the process plausibly takes;
- whether the setting has redundant safeguards the plot has ignored.

Do not use “the system is corrupt” as a universal permission. Specify the compromised person, access, action, and limit.

## 4. Information boundaries

For every reveal and line of dialogue, ask:

- Has this event happened for the speaker yet?
- Did the speaker witness it, infer it, receive it, or learn it from a record?
- Is the inference stronger than the available evidence?
- Does the wording accidentally reveal the author's knowledge?
- If the speaker is lying, what true information lets them construct the lie?

Knowledge leakage is a hard error. Fix the scene, not merely the wording.

## 5. Motive and proportionality

Check that:

- the benefit justifies the risk;
- the plan targets the right person;
- a lesser action would not solve the antagonist's problem more safely;
- accomplices receive a believable benefit or pressure;
- coerced characters still make choices consistent with their temperament;
- later cruelty does not exceed what earlier behavior prepared the reader to accept.

## 6. Antagonist competence

Ask what a cautious opponent would do after each protagonist move.

- preserve or destroy records;
- change routes or methods;
- test loyalty;
- create a counter-narrative;
- sacrifice an expendable accomplice;
- avoid explicit written or spoken admissions.

Give the opponent only capabilities already established. Do not grant omniscience.

## 7. Evidence and proof

When the resolution uses proof, track:

- origin;
- lawful or believable acquisition;
- custody and opportunity for tampering;
- independent corroboration;
- the strongest innocent counter-explanation;
- what the proof establishes and what it does not.

Avoid perfectly labeled confession files unless the character has a specific reason to preserve them, such as leverage, bookkeeping, or self-protection.

## 8. Emotion and aftermath

After death, injury, betrayal, public disgrace, or violence, check whether the character:

- first addresses immediate safety;
- shows a relationship-specific response;
- carries the event into later choices;
- avoids implausibly quick emotional recovery;
- retains conflicting feelings when appropriate.

Do not confuse emotional realism with longer description. One delayed action or concrete mistake can be enough.

## 9. Twist audit

For each proposed twist, answer:

- Which earlier clues support it?
- Why was the earlier interpretation reasonable?
- What changes after the reveal?
- Does any character need knowledge they could not possess?
- Does the twist preserve established motive and temperament?

Delete the twist if its main purpose is surprise or if it only changes the villain's label.

## 10. Clue ledger

Track every load-bearing clue:

| Clue | First appearance | Initial meaning | Later use | Final resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Remove decorative clues that imitate importance but never affect a decision. Add a fair setup for any object, skill, rule, or relationship required in the climax.

## 11. Ending audit

Confirm that:

- the protagonist's final move follows from earlier learning;
- the antagonist does not suddenly become careless;
- the climax works without a forced confession;
- the central external conflict is resolved on-page;
- the emotional ending reflects an actual change;
- the last image is not followed by a paragraph explaining its symbolism.

## 12. Compression pass

Delete or rewrite:

- repeated explanations of the same clue;
- paragraphs that call the protagonist clever;
- rhetorical summaries after a scene already proves the point;
- dialogue that both characters already know and would not naturally say;
- generic fear, anger, sadness, or shock without behavioral consequence;
- chapter endings written only as quotable slogans.
